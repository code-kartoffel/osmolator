Iterative measured correction

After measuring a sample, enter the result and press Adjust. The app automatically accounts for the sample volume removed from the mix (50 µL by default), then calculates the correction for the remaining solution. All non-adjuster components are kept at their starting concentrations through repeated test–adjust cycles. For glucose, sucrose and trehalose, repeated measurements can also refine the effective sugar-stock concentration; salts and all other stocks remain trusted at their entered values.

Calculation details

Ideal is the textbook count of particles per litre. Predicted is per kg of water, which is what a freezing-point osmometer reports.

Salts use the Pitzer ion-interaction model at 25 °C with published binary ion-pair parameters. Same-charge and ternary mixed-electrolyte parameters are not invented when unavailable; mixtures that need missing interaction terms are flagged.

Glucose, sucrose, sorbitol, mannitol, glycerol and urea are calculated from the published Norrish water-activity equation and published constants. Trehalose uses the sucrose Norrish constant because published measurements report nearly identical water-activity lowering at equal concentration. DMSO and ethanol retain the original approximate coefficients. PEG retains the original virial estimate and is explicitly marked approximate.

Concentrations per litre are converted to per kg of water using apparent molar volumes. Buffers are split into acid and base forms at the pH you set, with ionic-strength-corrected pKa values at 25 °C. Pipetting volumes assume stocks and water add up to the final volume. Specific salt–neutral cross-interactions are not added without published parameters; concentrated mixed solutions are therefore flagged. Temperature is fixed at 25 °C.
